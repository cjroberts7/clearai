A new kind of threat

AI moved faster than the law.
The law is catching up.

Businesses spent years getting GDPR right. AI has created the same scale of risk in a fraction of the time — and most organisations are completely unprepared.

The difference is, this time regulators aren't waiting. The EU AI Act is already in force. The ICO is already issuing AI guidance. The FCA is already asking financial firms how they govern AI. The enforcement wave is coming — and the businesses that acted early will be the ones that avoid it.

This isn't about being cautious. It's about being professional. Knowing your AI exposure and having the documentation to prove it is rapidly becoming the baseline expectation for any serious business.

The cost of doing nothing
EU AI Act fines reach €35 million. UK GDPR fines reach £17.5 million. FCA enforcement action can end a firm's authorisation. The question isn't whether you can afford to act — it's whether you can afford not to.
Most breaches start with shadow AI
Staff don't use unauthorised AI tools maliciously. They use them because they're useful. But client data fed into ChatGPT, financial records in Copilot, HR files in Gemini — that's a data breach waiting to be discovered.
Documentation is your first defence
When an incident happens — and it will — regulators ask one question first: did you have a documented AI policy? Businesses that can answer yes are in a fundamentally different position to those that can't.

The exposure vectors

What's already happening inside your business

Shadow AI in active use — ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini used daily by staff without policy, oversight, or IT awareness On average, businesses use 11+ AI tools — IT knows about fewer than 3 of them.
Multiple regulations already apply — UK GDPR, EU AI Act, FCA and US laws don't wait for you to be ready EU AI Act fines reach €35m. UK GDPR fines reach £17.5m. Enforcement has already started.
No policy means no defence — without documentation, every AI-related incident becomes a liability 78% of businesses have no written AI policy in place today.
Client data leaving your control — fed into third-party AI with no data processing agreement in place Sharing client data with AI tools, with no agreement in place, may already breach UK GDPR.
High-risk use cases unmanaged — AI in hiring, finance, and HR carries specific legal obligations most businesses don't know about HR and finance AI use is classed high-risk under the EU AI Act — extra documentation is required.
